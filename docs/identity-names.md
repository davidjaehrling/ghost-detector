# App name options (P1-06)

Owner: Copy & Localization · 2026-10-13 · Inputs: `docs/instrument-bible.md` §1 and §11, `docs/competitor-teardown.md`, `docs/MASTER-PLAN.md` §7, digest §4.

For Gate 1. Five store titles for Google Play, each with a subtitle direction. Title limit 30 characters, subtitle direction limit 80. Every subtitle states that readings are simulated. The final subtitle is rewritten at P9 against the live listing rules.

Collision checks are marked [U]. I cannot browse the store from this container, so every "could be confused with" list comes from the teardown and my knowledge of the category, and each needs a phone search before Gate 1 closes.

The device fiction behind every option: Velden Messtechnik GmbH, Wetzlar, model FS-4/87-P, built 1986 to 1989 (bible §1). The nameplate on the housing reads `VELDEN  FS-4/87-P`. The store name should match what a user sees on that plate, or at least not contradict it.

## Option 1. Velden FS-4 Field Probe (device-fiction)

- Title: `Velden FS-4 Field Probe` (23 characters)
- Subtitle direction: `Simulated field instrument. All readings are generated in software.` (67)
- Rationale: The title is the nameplate. A user who opens the app sees the same words engraved on the housing, so the store page and the product agree. EN reads it as a piece of survey gear. DE hears a real manufacturer: "Velden" and "Feldsonde" sound like a Wetzlar catalogue entry, and the audience will not take it for a joke. ES and PT read "Field Probe" as an English technical label, which is normal for instruments in both markets, and nothing in it mistranslates. The tone is instrument all the way: no ghost word appears in the title, so there is no prank signal at all. The cost is discovery. Nobody searches for Velden, so findability rests on the subtitle and the listing text.
- Pronounceability and typing: "Velden" is said the same way in all four locales (V as in DE "f" for Germans, as "v" elsewhere, which is harmless). The hyphen and digit in "FS-4" cost two extra key switches on a phone keyboard. Said aloud: "Velden F-S-four".
- Play-store collision check [U]: no known app with this title. Generic "field probe" and "EMF meter" listings exist in the EMF clone family (teardown §4), but none uses a manufacturer name. Search "Velden" on a phone to see if a real company or a game uses it.
- Compliance self-check: no "detector" in the title or subtitle. The subtitle says simulated and generated in software. No claim of finding, sensing or proving anything. Pass.

## Option 2. Ghost Detector: FS-4 Simulator (descriptive-category, keeps the searchable phrase)

- Title: `Ghost Detector: FS-4 Simulator` (30 characters, exactly at the limit)
- Subtitle direction: `Simulation only. Every reading is generated in software. Detects nothing.` (73)
- Rationale: This is the option that people can find. "Ghost Detector" is the phrase the category searches on, and the title keeps it as the first two words. The word "Simulator" in the same line, and a subtitle that says the unit detects nothing, turns the phrase from a claim into a label for a simulation. That is the only reading of "detector" Compliance can accept in a title. In EN it is plain and a little dry, which suits the field manual voice. DE users search "Geister Detektor" or "Ghost Detector" and will find the English phrase; "Simulator" is a known loanword in DE. ES and PT read "Simulator" as "Simulador", close enough to be understood on sight. The risk is tone: sitting next to Zeepa's "Ghost Detector Entity Radar", the first two words put us in the clone family before the user reads on. The subtitle and the icon must carry the difference.
- Pronounceability and typing: easy to say, "Ghost Detector", then "F-S-four Simulator". The colon and hyphen add two symbol-layer taps on a phone. At 30 characters it fills the title line, and Play may truncate on small cards.
- Play-store collision check [U]: high. "Ghost Detector Entity Radar" (Zeepa, about 2.5M installs, teardown §1) shares the first two words. Many other listings use "Ghost Detector" with a suffix. Expect search results to put us beside them. A user could mistake us for that app family, which is why the title says Simulator.
- Compliance self-check: "Detector" is in the title, so it is acceptable only because the subtitle states simulation and "detects nothing". The subtitle contains the word "detects" in a negation of the claim, which is the sticker wording (bible §11). Reviewer should confirm Play's metadata check reads the negation as intended. If Compliance judges the title word too risky, fall back to Option 3 or 5. Conditional pass.

## Option 3. Residual Field (evocative)

- Title: `Residual Field` (14 characters)
- Subtitle direction: `Simulated paranormal field meter. All readings are generated for entertainment.` (79)
- Rationale: "Residual" is one of the entity classes in the voice guide (`Class: RESIDUAL`) and "field" is the unit the instrument reports (flux, in mG). The two words together read like a measurement term and a quiet hint at something left behind. In EN it sounds like a physics term, which fits the instrument. DE: "Residual" is understood from "Residuum" but is not a common word, so DE needs the subtitle to do the work. ES "residual" and PT "residual" are ordinary words (leftover, residue), and both read as technical. The name carries no ghost word, so the store search will not surface it; discovery rests on the subtitle's "paranormal". Tone is quiet and serious, which is the point of the whole concept. It is one step away from the Velden fiction, since the device does not appear in the name.
- Pronounceability and typing: two common words, no symbols, 14 characters, easy on any keyboard. Said aloud: "Residual Field". Stress falls differently in ES and PT but is natural in each.
- Play-store collision check [U]: "Residual" and "Field" are common words, so partial matches are near certain (physics, farming and finance apps). No known paranormal app of this exact name in the teardown. Phone search needed.
- Compliance self-check: no "detector" anywhere. The subtitle says simulated and generated. "Paranormal field meter" names the genre and the subtitle's first word says it is simulated. No claim of real detection. Pass.

## Option 4. SPI-87 Presence Index (acronym or code)

- Title: `SPI-87 Presence Index` (21 characters)
- Subtitle direction: `Simulated instrument. Presence index and readings are generated in software.` (76)
- Rationale: SPI is the Spectral Presence Index, the 0 to 100 composite the device shows (bible §1 and §3). "87" is the model year of the FS-4/87. The title is the headline number of the instrument, so a screenshot of a reading and the store name are the same thing. In EN it reads as a lab designation. DE reads it as a standard or a norm number, as in DIN 87, which is exactly the authority register the voice wants. ES and PT see a code plus a plain word ("Índice de presença" in PT is close). It signals instrument at the highest level of any option. It is also the weakest in plain language: a stranger cannot say what it is, so the subtitle has to carry the whole job, and the title gives no search hook.
- Pronounceability and typing: "S-P-I eighty-seven" is easy to say and easy to remember as a code. Hyphen and digits cost three taps on the symbol and number rows. In PT "SPI" has no clash. In ES "spi" is not a word.
- Play-store collision check [U]: "SPI" collides with the serial bus protocol (SPI), with SPI index apps for finance and weather (Standardized Precipitation Index), and with a few sports apps. None known as a ghost app. The code needs a phone search for "SPI-87".
- Compliance self-check: no "detector". The subtitle says simulated and generated in software. The title names an index the app computes, which is true. Pass.

## Option 5. Velden Ghost Meter Simulator (hybrid)

- Title: `Velden Ghost Meter Simulator` (28 characters)
- Subtitle direction: `Simulated 1987-style field meter. Every reading is generated in software.` (73)
- Rationale: It joins the manufacturer from the plate with the category word and the honesty word. "Ghost Meter" is what a search for "ghost detector" or "ghost meter" can match without claiming detection, and "Simulator" states the nature of the product in the title itself. The Velden name keeps the fiction that the whole product is built on, so a user who sees the nameplate inside the app finds the same name in the store. EN reads it as a brand plus a plain product description. DE hears "Velden" as a German maker and "Simulator" as a loanword, so it reads straight. ES and PT: "Ghost" is understood from games and film, "Meter" reads as an English label, and "Simulator" lands as "Simulador". The tone is instrument, with the simulation said first. The name is long, and "Velden" is unknown in the market, so it ranks lower than Option 2 for exact-phrase search.
- Pronounceability and typing: four easy words, no symbols. Said aloud: "Velden Ghost Meter Simulator", about as long as a person will repeat. On a phone it is one line of typing without symbol taps.
- Play-store collision check [U]: "Ghost Meter" and "Ghost Meter Simulator" style names exist in the clone family (EMF and spirit meter listings, teardown §4). "Velden" alone has no known app. Combined title is likely unique, but a search for "ghost meter" will show close neighbours.
- Compliance self-check: no "detector". Subtitle says simulated and generated in software. "Simulator" in the title states the nature before the subtitle does. Pass.
